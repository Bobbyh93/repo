# Harrity Lesson Blueprint

## Design Intent

A Harrity lesson package is a high-level map plus drill-down system. It starts by showing the whole clinical terrain, then uses performance data and learner questions to decide where to zoom in. It is built for nursing education, especially chapters where biology must become bedside judgment.

## Core Pattern

Use this pattern unless the user supplies a stronger one:

1. **Opening patient question** - a short clinical situation that makes the content matter.
2. **Organizing clinical question** - one big question that controls the chapter.
3. **Concept lanes** - 4 to 6 boxes that organize the chapter map.
4. **Chapter map** - a route from foundation to clinical decision.
5. **Foundation activation** - a sequence, match, sort, or predict task.
6. **Structure-function reasoning** - why the body part, process, lab, or system produces the cue.
7. **Timeline or process model** - what happens first, next, and later.
8. **Checkpoint** - one targeted question tied to a common misconception.
9. **Patient application** - patient teaching, risk recognition, or escalation decision.
10. **Clinical judgment drill** - cue recognition, analysis, prioritization, solution, action, or evaluation.
11. **Capstone case** - a safe next action or priority decision.
12. **Debrief** - why the correct action is safe and why the distractors are unsafe or incomplete.
13. **Exit retrieval** - 3 to 5 prompts that reveal remaining gaps.
14. **Takeaway** - a concise bedside action frame.

## Visual Style Rules

- Light neutral background.
- One clear title at top left.
- Short module label above the title.
- Rounded cards with colored accent bars.
- Horizontal maps for time, process, or escalation.
- Color lanes consistently across the deck.
- Use minimal text. Prefer short phrases that can be spoken around.
- Put the clinical nursing message in a contrasting callout near the bottom.
- Avoid dense bullet slides unless the slide is a retrieval checklist.

## Slide Title Style

Use titles that state the clinical point:

- Weak: `Hormones`
- Better: `Hormones: think control room, not flashcards`

- Weak: `Placenta`
- Better: `Placenta: exchange organ, not a shield`

- Weak: `Teratogens`
- Better: `Teratogen risk engine: do not reduce risk to one variable`

## Concept Lane Examples

Early pregnancy default:

- **Create** - anatomy, gametes, conception, zygote.
- **Support** - hormones, placenta, membranes, cord, circulation.
- **Protect** - timing, development periods, barriers, teratogens.
- **Signal** - bleeding, pain, fever, symptoms, abnormal cue recognition.

Generic nursing-topic lanes:

- **Normal** - what should happen.
- **Cue** - what the nurse can observe, measure, or hear.
- **Meaning** - what the cue suggests physiologically.
- **Risk** - what can harm the patient first.
- **Action** - what the nurse teaches, assesses, escalates, or documents.

## Drill-Down Rule

The high-level map is not decorative. Use it as an index. When performance data or questions show a weak point, return to the map, mark the lane, and insert the smallest targeted drill that fixes the missed thinking operation.

## Slide-by-slide

### S01 · Recognition of an Acute Transfusion Reaction
*title · cross-lane · instructor-added · 60 s*  
**Objective:** Orient to the lesson's promise.  

**Script:**

Recognition of an Acute Transfusion Reaction. The concept underneath it is complication recognition, and the module rates its safety risk high — its top rating, so this is one to be able to do under pressure rather than one to recognise on a test. Here is the whole lesson in one sentence. Recognize new symptoms during transfusion, stop the blood product, maintain access with appropriate compatible fluid and tubing per policy, assess, and notify. By the end you should be able to do three things: recognize findings associated with a possible transfusion reaction; sequence immediate nursing actions; and complete required notification and specimen/product handling. We get there in three moves, and then spend the second half putting all of them against patient cues, because knowing this and doing it at the bedside are not the same skill.

### S06 · Recognize
*concept_cards · recognize · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Recognize findings associated with a possible transfusion reaction.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Recognize. Treat new fever, chills, dyspnea, chest or back discomfort, hypotension, urticaria, or other acute change during transfusion as a possible reaction until evaluated. This is the first of the three, and the two after it assume it. What you get wrong here does not stay here; it propagates quietly into every decision the rest of the lesson asks you to make. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can recognize findings associated with a possible transfusion reaction, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S07 · Stop and assess
*concept_cards · stop and assess · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Sequence immediate nursing actions.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Stop and assess. Stop the transfusion immediately, maintain venous access using new tubing and compatible solution according to policy, assess the patient, and obtain vital signs. This is the middle move, and it is where most of the disagreement in this lesson will sit: the point at which a defensible action and the priority action stop being the same answer. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can sequence immediate nursing actions, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S08 · Notify and investigate
*concept_cards · notify and investigate · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Complete required notification and specimen/product handling.  
**CJM:** take action, evaluate outcomes  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Notify and investigate. Notify the provider and transfusion service, follow emergency orders, and send the product, tubing, or specimens as required by organizational policy. This is the last of the three, and it is the one most often left implicit -- the step learners can describe accurately and then do not actually perform when the first two have gone well. Say it out loud; that is how it survives contact with a busy shift. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can complete required notification and specimen/product handling, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S09 · Activity: match the cue to the move it calls for
*match_activity · apply · source-aligned · 170 s*  
**Objective:** Match each cue to the lesson move that answers it.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Work in pairs, four minutes. Every cue on the left was written for one of the lesson moves on the right; your job is to say which, and to be able to defend one of the four out loud. Where the two of you match a cue differently, stop and stay there — you have found a cue whose lane is genuinely arguable, and those are worth more than the three you agreed on instantly. Bring one disagreement to the debrief.

**Activity:** Write the four matches and one sentence explaining one of them.

**Answer key:**
- A relates to: Recognize
- B relates to: Stop and assess
- C relates to: Notify and investigate
- D relates to: Notify and investigate

### S10 · Debrief: the reasoning behind each action
*debrief · apply · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Explain why each keyed action follows from its cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Read these four rationales as a set rather than one at a time. They are deliberately repetitive: the same habit of mind is showing up behind four different cues, and that repetition is the teaching point rather than an accident of writing. A learner who memorises four rules will meet a fifth cue and stall. A learner who can name the one habit will handle cues this lesson never showed them. Say the habit out loud in your own words before you leave this slide.

### S12 · Mini case
*mini_case · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Respond to a cue using the lesson's frame.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, take action  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

A bedside moment, and this time you write rather than choose. The situation: The patient's blood pressure is falling during the reaction. Write two sentences before the debrief: one naming what you assess, one naming what you do about it. Keep them in that order, because the order is the judgement. The keyed reasoning is: Hemodynamic instability requires immediate escalation and resuscitative priorities.

**Activity:** Two sentences: what you assess, then what you do.

**Answer key:**
- Activate urgent assistance and support airway, breathing, and circulation while following emergency orders.
- Hemodynamic instability requires immediate escalation and resuscitative priorities.

### S13 · Sort by urgency
*urgency_sort · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*

**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Two columns, and the sorting rule is the point of the slide. Put a cue on the left when delay changes the outcome for the patient, and on the right when the correct action is verification, planning or teaching that a short delay does not worsen. Justify one of your placements aloud. Learners who can sort these reliably are the ones who prioritise well under load.

**Activity:** Place each cue and justify one.

**Answer key:**

## Assessment items

### Q01 · mcq · recognize · recognize cues
**Stem:** The patient develops chills and dyspnea shortly after a transfusion begins. What should the nurse do?  
- A. Stop the transfusion immediately and assess the patient.
- B. Delay action until the end of the shift.
- C. Document the finding without reassessment or follow-up.
- D. Delegate the clinical judgment decision to unlicensed assistive personnel.

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Stopping exposure and assessing stability are the immediate priorities for a suspected reaction.

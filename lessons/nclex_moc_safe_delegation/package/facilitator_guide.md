## Slide-by-slide

### S01 · Safe Delegation and Supervision
*title · cross-lane · instructor-added · 60 s*  
**Objective:** Orient to the lesson's promise.  

**Script:**

Safe Delegation and Supervision. The concept underneath it is care coordination, and the module rates its safety risk high — its top rating, so this is one to be able to do under pressure rather than one to recognise on a test. Here is the whole lesson in one sentence. Match the task to the delegatee, retain nursing judgment, communicate expectations, and evaluate the result. By the end you should be able to do three things: differentiate tasks that require nursing judgment from tasks that may be delegated; select an appropriate delegatee using patient stability, task predictability, and local scope; and provide direction, supervision, and outcome evaluation after delegation. We get there in three moves, and then spend the second half putting all of them against patient cues, because knowing this and doing it at the bedside are not the same skill.

### S06 · Decision frame
*concept_cards · decision frame · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Differentiate tasks that require nursing judgment from tasks that may be delegated.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Decision frame. The nurse assesses the patient and task before delegating. Assessment, teaching, evaluation, and unstable-patient decisions remain with the licensed nurse. This is the first of the three, and the two after it assume it. What you get wrong here does not stay here; it propagates quietly into every decision the rest of the lesson asks you to make. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can differentiate tasks that require nursing judgment from tasks that may be delegated, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S07 · Communication
*concept_cards · communication · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Select an appropriate delegatee using patient stability, task predictability, and local scope.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Communication. State the task, expected result, reporting parameters, and time frame. Confirm understanding and availability for questions. This is the middle move, and it is where most of the disagreement in this lesson will sit: the point at which a defensible action and the priority action stop being the same answer. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can select an appropriate delegatee using patient stability, task predictability, and local scope, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S08 · Supervision
*concept_cards · supervision · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Provide direction, supervision, and outcome evaluation after delegation.  
**CJM:** take action, evaluate outcomes  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Supervision. Delegation transfers performance of a task, not accountability for the nursing decision. Reassess the patient and evaluate completion. This is the last of the three, and it is the one most often left implicit -- the step learners can describe accurately and then do not actually perform when the first two have gone well. Say it out loud; that is how it survives contact with a busy shift. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can provide direction, supervision, and outcome evaluation after delegation, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S09 · Activity: match the cue to the move it calls for
*match_activity · apply · source-aligned · 170 s*  
**Objective:** Match each cue to the lesson move that answers it.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Work in pairs, four minutes. Every cue on the left was written for one of the lesson moves on the right; your job is to say which, and to be able to defend one of the four out loud. Where the two of you match a cue differently, stop and stay there — you have found a cue whose lane is genuinely arguable, and those are worth more than the three you agreed on instantly. Bring one disagreement to the debrief.

**Activity:** Write the four matches and one sentence explaining one of them.

**Answer key:**
- A relates to: Decision frame
- B relates to: Communication
- C relates to: Supervision
- D relates to: Supervision

### S10 · Debrief: the reasoning behind each action
*debrief · apply · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Explain why each keyed action follows from its cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Read these four rationales as a set rather than one at a time. They are deliberately repetitive: the same habit of mind is showing up behind four different cues, and that repetition is the teaching point rather than an accident of writing. A learner who memorises four rules will meet a fifth cue and stall. A learner who can name the one habit will handle cues this lesson never showed them. Say the habit out loud in your own words before you leave this slide.

### S11 · Checkpoint
*checkpoint_mcq · communication · source-aligned · 120 s*  
**Objective:** Choose the correct action for a single cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

One question, and it targets the middle of the lesson. The cue is: A stable patient needs routine intake and output recorded. Read all four options and commit to one before the reveal, because the value of a checkpoint is in being wrong out loud rather than quietly. The reasoning you are being tested on is this: A predictable task may be delegated when direction and supervision are provided.

**Activity:** Choose the best response.

**Answer key:**
- C. Delegate measurement with clear reporting parameters and review the result.

### S12 · Mini case
*mini_case · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Respond to a cue using the lesson's frame.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, take action  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

A bedside moment, and this time you write rather than choose. The situation: A patient requires initial teaching for a new diagnosis. Write two sentences before the debrief: one naming what you assess, one naming what you do about it. Keep them in that order, because the order is the judgement. The keyed reasoning is: Initial teaching and evaluation require nursing judgment.

**Activity:** Two sentences: what you assess, then what you do.

**Answer key:**
- The registered nurse performs and evaluates the initial teaching.
- Initial teaching and evaluation require nursing judgment.

### S13 · Sort by urgency
*urgency_sort · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Sort cues by whether they require immediate action.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Two columns, and the sorting rule is the point of the slide. Put a cue on the left when delay changes the outcome for the patient, and on the right when the correct action is verification, planning or teaching that a short delay does not worsen. Justify one of your placements aloud. Learners who can sort these reliably are the ones who prioritise well under load.

**Activity:** Place each cue and justify one.

**Answer key:**
- Act now: the cues whose keyed action is immediate
- Act in sequence: the cues whose keyed action is verification or planning

### S15 · Capstone: choose the next safe action
*capstone_mcq · evaluate · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Select the priority action in the lesson's hardest cue.  
**CJM:** take action, evaluate outcomes  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

The last question, and the hardest cue the topic carries: The task is permitted by policy but the delegatee has not demonstrated competence. All four options are things a nurse might plausibly do, which is what makes it a capstone rather than a checkpoint. Choose the priority action, not merely a defensible one. The reasoning that separates them: Permitted scope does not replace validation of individual competence.

**Activity:** Choose the priority action.

**Answer key:**
- B. Assign the task to a competent team member or complete it as the nurse.

## Assessment items

### Q01 · mcq · decision frame · recognize cues
**Stem:** A newly admitted patient has changing neurologic findings. What should the nurse do?  
- A. The registered nurse performs the focused assessment and determines the next intervention.
- B. Delay action until the end of the shift.
- C. Document the finding without reassessment or follow-up.
- D. Delegate the clinical judgment decision to unlicensed assistive personnel.

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** An unstable or changing condition requires licensed nursing assessment and judgment.

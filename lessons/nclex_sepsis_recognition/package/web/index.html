Baseline: what perfusion is for

Explain why a perfusion failure shows up in several systems at once.

  • Perfusion delivers oxygen to tissue
  • Organs announce failure in their own language
  • Compare against this patient's own trend, not a textbook number

Brain Altered mentation: new confusion, less responsive than earlier.

Kidney Reduced urine output.

Lungs Respiratory change.

Circulation Abnormal perfusion; hypotension.

Cues: the five the module names

cues · source-aligned · sources SRC02

Name the cue families that, with possible infection, require urgent assessment.

  • Possible infection PLUS any new organ dysfunction
  • Altered mentation · abnormal perfusion · respiratory change
  • Hypotension · reduced urine output
  • Any other new organ dysfunction counts

Altered mentation New confusion or reduced responsiveness.

Abnormal perfusion Skin and circulatory findings that differ from this patient's earlier state.

Respiratory change A new change in the work or rate of breathing.

Hypotension Blood pressure below this patient's trend.

Reduced urine output Less urine than earlier in the shift.

Other new organ dysfunction The list is not a closed set.

Trend beats any single value

meaning · source-aligned · sources SRC02

Justify using the trend rather than one reading.

  • One value is a snapshot; the trend is the story
  • Combined movement in several systems raises urgency
  • A value inside the normal range can still be a change

single value a snapshot; may sit inside the normal range

two values direction becomes visible

several systems combined movement suggests worsening perfusion

urgency direction, not the number, decides

Checkpoint: the patient with a normal temperature

cues · source-aligned · sources SRC02

Reject the assumption that a normal temperature excludes sepsis.

A patient has a possible infection but a temperature within the normal range. What is the best nursing response?

  1. Document the normal temperature and recheck at the next scheduled vitals.
  2. Rule out sepsis, since fever is required for the diagnosis.
  3. Continue evaluating for sepsis using the complete clinical picture and organ-function findings.
  4. Wait for the culture result before assessing further.
Try it: Choose the best response.

Meaning: new organ dysfunction raises urgency

meaning · source-aligned · sources SRC02

State the rule that converts a cue into an escalation.

  • Possible infection + new organ dysfunction = urgent
  • The dysfunction does not have to be severe, only new
  • Absence of fever does not lower the urgency

New Different from this patient's own earlier state.

Organ dysfunction Any system reporting a perfusion failure.

Together With possible infection, the two together mean urgent assessment now.

Escalation and stabilization happen together

Reject the sequential reading in which one step waits for another.

  • Deterioration requires parallel action
  • Do not wait passively for one step to finish
  • Diagnostics and treatment are coordinated, not queued

activate + communicate pathway and trend, together

diagnostics + stabilization coordinated, not queued

Mini case: the patient who does not improve

evaluate · source-aligned · sources SRC02

Choose the response when the first intervention has not worked.

Blood pressure remains low after initial interventions for suspected sepsis.

  • Assess: perfusion, mentation, urine output, and the full trend since the intervention
  • Escalate: report immediately that hypotension persists; keep reassessing response
  • Avoid: reading a completed intervention as a resolved problem

Capstone: choose the next safe action

evaluate · source-aligned · sources SRC02

Select the priority response to persistent hypotension after intervention.

What is the priority nursing action?

  1. Recheck the blood pressure in thirty minutes to confirm the trend before calling.
  2. Escalate immediately and continue reassessing perfusion and treatment response.
  3. Document that interventions were completed as ordered and continue the current plan.
  4. Reposition the patient and repeat the reading in the other arm before escalating.

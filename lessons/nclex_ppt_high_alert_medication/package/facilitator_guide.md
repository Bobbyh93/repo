## Slide-by-slide

### S01 · High-Alert Medication Administration
*title · cross-lane · instructor-added · 60 s*  
**Objective:** Orient to the lesson's promise.  

**Script:**

High-Alert Medication Administration. The concept underneath it is medication safety, and the module rates its safety risk high — its top rating, so this is one to be able to do under pressure rather than one to recognise on a test. Here is the whole lesson in one sentence. Verify the complete medication order, patient-specific parameters, independent checks when required, monitoring, and response to harm. By the end you should be able to do three things: identify safeguards for high-alert medications; recognize parameters that require holding and clarification; and monitor for therapeutic and adverse effects. We get there in three moves, and then spend the second half putting all of them against patient cues, because knowing this and doing it at the bedside are not the same skill.

**CJM:** recognize cues

**Script:**

We start with a cue rather than a definition, because that is the order the unit gives you: the patient arrives first and the explanation catches up later. Here it is. The prescribed dose is inconsistent with the available concentration and usual range. Decide now, before any of this is taught, what you would do and why that thing first. Write it somewhere you will still be able to read at the end, because this exact cue comes back as the first assessment item. Comparing the two answers is most of what the rest of this session is for.

### S06 · Before administration
*concept_cards · before administration · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Identify safeguards for high-alert medications.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Before administration. Verify patient identity, medication, indication, dose, route, time, allergies, relevant laboratory data, interactions, and required independent checks. This is the first of the three, and the two after it assume it. What you get wrong here does not stay here; it propagates quietly into every decision the rest of the lesson asks you to make. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can identify safeguards for high-alert medications, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S07 · During administration
*concept_cards · during administration · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Recognize parameters that require holding and clarification.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

During administration. Use the correct equipment and rate, minimize interruptions, and do not bypass smart-device or barcode safeguards without resolving the cause. This is the middle move, and it is where most of the disagreement in this lesson will sit: the point at which a defensible action and the priority action stop being the same answer. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can recognize parameters that require holding and clarification, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S08 · After administration
*concept_cards · after administration · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Monitor for therapeutic and adverse effects.  
**CJM:** take action, evaluate outcomes  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

After administration. Monitor the expected effect and predictable harms, document the response, and act immediately on evidence of an adverse event. This is the last of the three, and it is the one most often left implicit -- the step learners can describe accurately and then do not actually perform when the first two have gone well. Say it out loud; that is how it survives contact with a busy shift. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can monitor for therapeutic and adverse effects, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S09 · Activity: match the cue to the move it calls for
*match_activity · apply · source-aligned · 170 s*  
**Objective:** Match each cue to the lesson move that answers it.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Work in pairs, four minutes. Every cue on the left was written for one of the lesson moves on the right; your job is to say which, and to be able to defend one of the four out loud. Where the two of you match a cue differently, stop and stay there — you have found a cue whose lane is genuinely arguable, and those are worth more than the three you agreed on instantly. Bring one disagreement to the debrief.

**Activity:** Write the four matches and one sentence explaining one of them.

**Answer key:**
- A relates to: Before administration
- B relates to: During administration
- C relates to: After administration
- D relates to: After administration

### S10 · Debrief: the reasoning behind each action
*debrief · apply · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Explain why each keyed action follows from its cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Read these four rationales as a set rather than one at a time. They are deliberately repetitive: the same habit of mind is showing up behind four different cues, and that repetition is the teaching point rather than an accident of writing. A learner who memorises four rules will meet a fifth cue and stall. A learner who can name the one habit will handle cues this lesson never showed them. Say the habit out loud in your own words before you leave this slide.

### S11 · Checkpoint
*checkpoint_mcq · during administration · source-aligned · 120 s*  
**Objective:** Choose the correct action for a single cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

One question, and it targets the middle of the lesson. The cue is: A required patient-specific laboratory result is unavailable. Read all four options and commit to one before the reveal, because the value of a checkpoint is in being wrong out loud rather than quietly. The reasoning you are being tested on is this: Relevant laboratory data can determine whether administration is safe.

**Activity:** Choose the best response.

**Answer key:**
- C. Obtain or verify the required result before administering the medication.

### S12 · Mini case
*mini_case · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Respond to a cue using the lesson's frame.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, take action  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

A bedside moment, and this time you write rather than choose. The situation: Barcode scanning produces a mismatch alert. Write two sentences before the debrief: one naming what you assess, one naming what you do about it. Keep them in that order, because the order is the judgement. The keyed reasoning is: A mismatch alert is a safety signal and should not be bypassed for convenience.

**Activity:** Two sentences: what you assess, then what you do.

**Answer key:**
- Stop and resolve the patient, medication, and order mismatch.
- A mismatch alert is a safety signal and should not be bypassed for convenience.

### S13 · Sort by urgency
*urgency_sort · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Sort cues by whether they require immediate action.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Two columns, and the sorting rule is the point of the slide. Put a cue on the left when delay changes the outcome for the patient, and on the right when the correct action is verification, planning or teaching that a short delay does not worsen. Justify one of your placements aloud. Learners who can sort these reliably are the ones who prioritise well under load.

**Activity:** Place each cue and justify one.

**Answer key:**
- Act now: the cues whose keyed action is immediate
- Act in sequence: the cues whose keyed action is verification or planning

### S15 · Capstone: choose the next safe action
*capstone_mcq · evaluate · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Select the priority action in the lesson's hardest cue.  
**CJM:** take action, evaluate outcomes  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

The last question, and the hardest cue the topic carries: The patient develops signs of an acute adverse reaction during administration. All four options are things a nurse might plausibly do, which is what makes it a capstone rather than a checkpoint. Choose the priority action, not merely a defensible one. The reasoning that separates them: Immediate assessment and response limit ongoing exposure and address instability.

**Activity:** Choose the priority action.

**Answer key:**
- B. Stop the medication when appropriate, assess the patient, and activate the emergency response pathway.

## Assessment items

### Q01 · mcq · before administration · recognize cues
**Stem:** The prescribed dose is inconsistent with the available concentration and usual range. What should the nurse do?  
- A. Hold administration and clarify the order before preparing the dose.
- B. Delay action until the end of the shift.
- C. Document the finding without reassessment or follow-up.
- D. Delegate the clinical judgment decision to unlicensed assistive personnel.

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** A dose discrepancy must be resolved before medication reaches the patient.  
*remediation → S06 · source-aligned · sources SRC02*

- C. Document the finding without reassessment or follow-up.

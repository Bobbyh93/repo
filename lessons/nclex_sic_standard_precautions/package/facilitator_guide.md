## Slide-by-slide

### S01 · Standard Precautions and Exposure Prevention
*title · cross-lane · instructor-added · 60 s*  
**Objective:** Orient to the lesson's promise.  

**Script:**

Standard Precautions and Exposure Prevention. The concept underneath it is infection prevention, and the module rates its safety risk high — its top rating, so this is one to be able to do under pressure rather than one to recognise on a test. Here is the whole lesson in one sentence. Select hand hygiene and protective equipment from the anticipated exposure, then remove equipment without contaminating clean areas. By the end you should be able to do three things: apply standard precautions to every patient encounter; select PPE from the anticipated exposure; and respond safely to a sharps or body-fluid exposure. We get there in three moves, and then spend the second half putting all of them against patient cues, because knowing this and doing it at the bedside are not the same skill.

### S06 · Anticipate exposure
*concept_cards · anticipate exposure · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Apply standard precautions to every patient encounter.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Anticipate exposure. Standard precautions apply to every patient. Add gloves, gown, mask, and eye protection according to likely contact with blood, body fluids, mucous membranes, nonintact skin, or contaminated equipment. This is the first of the three, and the two after it assume it. What you get wrong here does not stay here; it propagates quietly into every decision the rest of the lesson asks you to make. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can apply standard precautions to every patient encounter, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S07 · Hand hygiene
*concept_cards · hand hygiene · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Select PPE from the anticipated exposure.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Hand hygiene. Perform hand hygiene at indicated moments and after glove removal. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene. This is the middle move, and it is where most of the disagreement in this lesson will sit: the point at which a defensible action and the priority action stop being the same answer. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can select PPE from the anticipated exposure, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S08 · Exposure response
*concept_cards · exposure response · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Respond safely to a sharps or body-fluid exposure.  
**CJM:** take action, evaluate outcomes  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Exposure response. Immediately wash or flush the exposed area, report the event, and follow the organization's exposure evaluation process. This is the last of the three, and it is the one most often left implicit -- the step learners can describe accurately and then do not actually perform when the first two have gone well. Say it out loud; that is how it survives contact with a busy shift. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can respond safely to a sharps or body-fluid exposure, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S09 · Activity: match the cue to the move it calls for
*match_activity · apply · source-aligned · 170 s*  
**Objective:** Match each cue to the lesson move that answers it.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Work in pairs, four minutes. Every cue on the left was written for one of the lesson moves on the right; your job is to say which, and to be able to defend one of the four out loud. Where the two of you match a cue differently, stop and stay there — you have found a cue whose lane is genuinely arguable, and those are worth more than the three you agreed on instantly. Bring one disagreement to the debrief.

### S10 · Debrief: the reasoning behind each action
*debrief · apply · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Explain why each keyed action follows from its cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Read these four rationales as a set rather than one at a time. They are deliberately repetitive: the same habit of mind is showing up behind four different cues, and that repetition is the teaching point rather than an accident of writing. A learner who memorises four rules will meet a fifth cue and stall. A learner who can name the one habit will handle cues this lesson never showed them. Say the habit out loud in your own words before you leave this slide.

### S11 · Checkpoint
*checkpoint_mcq · hand hygiene · source-aligned · 120 s*  
**Objective:** Choose the correct action for a single cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

One question, and it targets the middle of the lesson. The cue is: Gloves are removed after contact with contaminated material. Read all four options and commit to one before the reveal, because the value of a checkpoint is in being wrong out loud rather than quietly. ### S12 · Mini case
*mini_case · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Respond to a cue using the lesson's frame.

**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

A bedside moment, and this time you write rather than choose. The situation: A nurse sustains a needlestick injury. Write two sentences before the debrief: one naming what you assess, one naming what you do about it. Keep them in that order, because the order is the judgement. The keyed reasoning is: Immediate cleansing and timely evaluation reduce preventable harm and enable indicated prophylaxis.

**Activity:** Two sentences: what you assess, then what you do.

### S13 · Sort by urgency
*urgency_sort · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Sort cues by whether they require immediate action.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Two columns, and the sorting rule is the point of the slide. Put a cue on the left when delay changes the outcome for the patient, and on the right when the correct action is verification, planning or teaching that a short delay does not worsen. Justify one of your placements aloud. Learners who can sort these reliably are the ones who prioritise well under load.

**Activity:** Place each cue and justify one.

**Answer key:**
- Act now: the cues whose keyed action is immediate
- Act in sequence: the cues whose keyed action is verification or planning

## Assessment items

### Q01 · mcq · anticipate exposure · recognize cues
**Stem:** A procedure may splash body fluid toward the face. What should the nurse do?  
- A. Use gloves plus facial and eye protection appropriate to the splash risk.
- B. Delay action until the end of the shift.
- C. Document the finding without reassessment or follow-up.
- D. Delegate the clinical judgment decision to unlicensed assistive personnel.

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** PPE selection is based on the anticipated route and extent of exposure.

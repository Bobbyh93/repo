## Slide-by-slide

### S01 · Pressure Injury Prevention and Comfort
*title · cross-lane · instructor-added · 60 s*  
**Objective:** Orient to the lesson's promise.  

**Script:**

Pressure Injury Prevention and Comfort. The concept underneath it is mobility and skin integrity, and the module rates its safety risk elevated — below the top rating, but high enough to be singled out from everything else in its category. Here is the whole lesson in one sentence. Identify individual pressure-injury risks and combine repositioning, surface selection, moisture management, nutrition, and skin reassessment. By the end you should be able to do three things: identify modifiable pressure-injury risks; select individualized prevention interventions; and evaluate skin response and update the plan. We get there in three moves, and then spend the second half putting all of them against patient cues, because knowing this and doing it at the bedside are not the same skill.

*opening_case · risk assessment · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Enter the lesson through a single patient cue.  
**CJM:** recognize cues

**Script:**

We start with a cue rather than a definition, because that is the order the unit gives you: the patient arrives first and the explanation catches up later. Here it is. Write it somewhere you will still be able to read at the end, because this exact cue comes back as the first assessment item. Comparing the two answers is most of what the rest of this session is for.

### S06 · Risk assessment
*concept_cards · risk assessment · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Identify modifiable pressure-injury risks.  
**CJM:** recognize cues, analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Risk assessment. Assess mobility, sensation, moisture, perfusion, nutrition, friction, shear, devices, and current skin findings. This is the first of the three, and the two after it assume it. What you get wrong here does not stay here; it propagates quietly into every decision the rest of the lesson asks you to make. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can identify modifiable pressure-injury risks, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S07 · Prevention bundle
*concept_cards · prevention bundle · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Select individualized prevention interventions.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Prevention bundle. Use individualized repositioning, pressure redistribution, heel off-loading, moisture protection, nutrition support, and device checks. This is the middle move, and it is where most of the disagreement in this lesson will sit: the point at which a defensible action and the priority action stop being the same answer. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can select individualized prevention interventions, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S08 · Evaluate
*concept_cards · evaluate · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Evaluate skin response and update the plan.  
**CJM:** take action, evaluate outcomes  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Evaluate. Inspect high-risk areas and device contact points, document changes, and revise the prevention plan when skin tolerance worsens. This is the last of the three, and it is the one most often left implicit -- the step learners can describe accurately and then do not actually perform when the first two have gone well. Say it out loud; that is how it survives contact with a busy shift. Do not move on until you can say this part back without the slide. The test is the objective: if you can evaluate skin response and update the plan, this lane has done its job, and if you cannot, nothing later in the lesson will fix it — it will only hide it.

### S09 · Activity: match the cue to the move it calls for

**Objective:** Match each cue to the lesson move that answers it.

**Sources:** SRC02

Work in pairs, four minutes. Every cue on the left was written for one of the lesson moves on the right; your job is to say which, and to be able to defend one of the four out loud. ### S10 · Debrief: the reasoning behind each action
*debrief · apply · source-aligned · 150 s*  
**Objective:** Explain why each keyed action follows from its cue.  
**CJM:** analyze cues  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Read these four rationales as a set rather than one at a time. They are deliberately repetitive: the same habit of mind is showing up behind four different cues, and that repetition is the teaching point rather than an accident of writing. A learner who memorises four rules will meet a fifth cue and stall. A learner who can name the one habit will handle cues this lesson never showed them. Say the habit out loud in your own words before you leave this slide.

### S11 · Checkpoint
*checkpoint_mcq · prevention bundle · source-aligned · 120 s*  
**Objective:** Choose the correct action for a single cue.

**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

One question, and it targets the middle of the lesson. The cue is: Moisture is trapped against the sacral skin. Read all four options and commit to one before the reveal, because the value of a checkpoint is in being wrong out loud rather than quietly. ### S12 · Mini case
*mini_case · apply · source-aligned · 165 s*  
**Objective:** Respond to a cue using the lesson's frame.  
**CJM:** prioritize hypotheses, take action  
**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

A bedside moment, and this time you write rather than choose. The situation: A medical device rests tightly against the patient's skin. Write two sentences before the debrief: one naming what you assess, one naming what you do about it. Keep them in that order, because the order is the judgement. The keyed reasoning is: Medical devices can concentrate pressure and conceal evolving injury.

**Activity:** Two sentences: what you assess, then what you do.

**Answer key:**
- Inspect beneath and around the device and relieve avoidable pressure.
- Medical devices can concentrate pressure and conceal evolving injury.

**Sources:** SRC02  

**Script:**

Two columns, and the sorting rule is the point of the slide. Put a cue on the left when delay changes the outcome for the patient, and on the right when the correct action is verification, planning or teaching that a short delay does not worsen. ## Assessment items

### Q01 · mcq · risk assessment · recognize cues
**Stem:** An immobile patient slides down repeatedly in bed. What should the nurse do?  
- A. Reposition with lift assistance and reduce shear while supporting alignment.
- B. Delay action until the end of the shift.
- C. Document the finding without reassessment or follow-up.
- D. Delegate the clinical judgment decision to unlicensed assistive personnel.

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** Dragging and sliding increase shear forces that damage tissue.
